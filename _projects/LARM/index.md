## Project Overview

As part of a multidisciplinary robotics project at **Texas A&M University**, I redesigned the lower arm assembly and syringe-based liquid-handling end effector of a **6-DOF Lab-Assisting Robotic Manipulator (LARM)**.

The project addressed structural weakness, inconsistent component fit, additive-manufacturing failures, and mechanical play at the end-effector interface. Using SolidWorks and Onshape, I developed revised components, produced engineering documentation, and validated the designs through multiple 3D-printed prototype iterations.

The completed prototypes achieved positioning accuracy within **±0.5 mm** while improving structural reliability, manufacturability, and component integration.

---

## Key Accomplishments

### Lower-Arm Redesign

Redesigned the lower arm to improve stiffness, reduce unnecessary material, strengthen mounting interfaces, and maintain joint clearance and range of motion.

### Syringe End Effector

Developed an end effector that securely retained and aligned a vaccine syringe for automated liquid-handling tasks. Refined the wrist interface to reduce mechanical play and support repeatable installation.

### Prototyping and Validation

Fabricated and tested multiple 3D-printed iterations for fit, motion, structural integrity, syringe retention, and component interoperability. Incorporated test observations into subsequent CAD revisions.

### Engineering Documentation

Produced detailed part drawings, assembly documentation, dimensions, tolerances, and manufacturing specifications to support repeatable fabrication and system integration.

### Project Outcome

Delivered an integrated lower-arm and syringe end-effector prototype with improved reliability and positioning repeatability, supporting faster wet-lab testing and design iteration.
