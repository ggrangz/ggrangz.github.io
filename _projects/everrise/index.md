## Project Overview

During my internship at **Everrise Engineering**, I designed and fabricated a 12-blade backward-curved centrifugal fan prototype. The project provided end-to-end experience in mechanical product development, from parametric CAD and manufacturing drawings through sheet-metal fabrication and final assembly.

The primary challenge was converting the impeller concept into a design compatible with the available laser-cutting, Amada press-brake, and spot-welding processes. Blade thickness, bend geometry, tolerances, and assembly interfaces had to be controlled while maintaining consistent positioning across all 12 blades.

Using Onshape, I developed the parametric CAD assembly and revised the sheet thickness from **1.0 mm to 1.6 mm and ultimately 1.2 mm** to balance stiffness, weight, formability, dimensional fit, and manufacturing practicality.

## Design Development

The fan assembly consisted of 12 backward-curved sheet-metal blades positioned between supporting impeller plates.

The CAD model incorporated:

- Parametric blade geometry
- Consistent angular spacing between 12 blades
- Sheet-metal bend requirements
- Blade-to-plate assembly interfaces
- Dimensional and fabrication tolerances
- Spot-welding access
- Hub and shaft interfaces
- Manufacturing and assembly clearances

Multiple CAD revisions were completed to refine blade curvature, bend location, material thickness, and component fit.

---

## Material-Thickness Iterations

| Design iteration | Sheet thickness | Evaluation |
|------------------|----------------:|------------|
| Initial design | 1.0 mm | Lightweight and formable, but required greater stiffness |
| Second design | 1.6 mm | Improved stiffness but increased weight and forming difficulty |
| Final design | 1.2 mm | Balanced stiffness, weight, formability, and assembly fit |

The final **1.2 mm sheet thickness** provided the best compromise between structural rigidity and practical fabrication.

## Engineering Insights

The project demonstrated that a complete CAD assembly is not automatically ready for production. Sheet thickness, bend allowance, tooling access, welding sequence, tolerances, and component alignment must be considered before fabrication begins.

Increasing material thickness improved blade stiffness but also increased weight and forming difficulty. The final design therefore represented a practical compromise between structural performance and manufacturability.

Consistent blade positioning was also critical because small alignment errors across the 12 blades could create assembly interference, geometric imbalance, and uneven aerodynamic performance.
